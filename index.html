The Helium Bottleneck

Global supply chain & the 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption

DISRUPTION

~27–30% of global helium supply offline. Qatar's Ras Laffan complex has been unable to export since March 2026 following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

About this bottleneck

Helium is a non-renewable byproduct of natural-gas processing. It is the second-lightest element and cannot be practically stockpiled: it leaks through most container materials and, once vented, escapes Earth's atmosphere into space entirely.

Production is concentrated in a handful of gas fields, and new purification plants take 5–7 years to design, permit and commission. There is no quick substitute for a lost source, which is why a single chokepoint can ripple through semiconductors, MRI scanners and hard drives for years. Qatar's damaged facilities alone are expected to take around a year to repair, even once the conflict ends.

End users adapt by recycling what they can: research institutions such as ETH Zurich now reuse roughly 85% of their helium (over 90% for sensitive NMR instruments). But recycling only stretches existing supply, it cannot replace a lost source.
